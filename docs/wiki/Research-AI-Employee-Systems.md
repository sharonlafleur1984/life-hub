# Research: AI employee systems for life admin

**Last updated:** October 4, 2026

**Read this when:** Before changing how the house is organized, how specialists run, what they cost, or before weighing any tool seen in an ad or course.

**How to read the tags:** [P] verified from a primary source, [S] a single secondary source, [I] an inference from the evidence. "Re-check" marks a figure that reached us through a summarizing fetch rather than the page itself. Raw research notes are not published; this page is the record.

---

Sharon's design is the right shape and nobody we could find runs a better one, but three of her four goals depend on details the current design leaves open, and the fourth (cheap) depends on not doing the one thing the "AI employees" framing invites, which is waking all eight specialists every day. The evidence converges on one rule: many agents may read, research and propose, but one agent must make the writes and the priority calls; parallel independent agents amplified errors 17.2x versus 4.4x for centralized coordination in a 180-configuration Google Research study ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)) [P]. "Work already done" is realistic for low-stakes items and must stop at a draft for calendar, school and money, because every surviving commercial assistant keeps an approval step and the one that tried full autonomy on family scheduling (Milo) shut down in January 2026 ([Milo](https://joinmilo.substack.com/p/hellogoodbye)) [P]. "Continuous learning" is real only as external notes a fresh run reads first and writes last, since cloud routines start a new session every time ([Routines docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)) [P]. "Tell her to shift priorities" has no documented working example anywhere; the closest evidence is that critics with explicit rubrics and clean context outperform ones anchored to the author's framing [S/I]. Cost is driven by tokens per run, not run count, so the cheapest version of her Hub is one daily routine on a cheap model that fans out only when a specialist's inputs changed. The advertised sellers leave almost no trail and sell the same stack she already has; OpenClaw is the same architecture with more reach and a serious security record; commercial household products are narrower than her plan and are dying, not winning. Borrow the engineering patterns, keep Notion and the Idea Bench, change the fan-out and add a goal-arbiter, and stop believing that a persona is a capability.

## One writer, parallel readers: the reorganization that matters

Her orchestrator-plus-eight-specialists is consistent with the 2026 consensus only under one condition: the house manager (or one designated agent) is the sole writer of priority changes, calendar entries and Notion decision records, and specialists return findings and proposals. Cognition's original warning was that actions carry implicit decisions and two agents with unshared context make conflicting ones ([Cognition, June 2025](https://cognition.com/blog/dont-build-multi-agents)) [P, vendor]; its April 2026 follow-up kept that line while conceding that reviewers and "intelligence contributors" with clean context work well ([Cognition, Apr 2026](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working)) [S, vendor]. The Google Research study is the independent anchor: centralized coordination gained 80.9% on a parallelizable finance task while every multi-agent variant lost 39% to 70% on a sequential planning task ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)) [P, preprint, not life-admin benchmarks]. Two specialists independently touching the family calendar or the budget is the documented failure case [I].

The Google Cloud post does not map cleanly onto Claude, and that matters for her terminology. Claude subagents run in isolated context and return a summary, which is Google's "agent-as-tool" behavior on the context axis, while Claude skills are instructions loaded into the same context and are not agents at all ([Subagents docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents), [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/where-to-use-sub-agents-versus-agents-as-tools/)) [P]. Google's useful test is stateful versus stateless: a specialist whose value is accumulated domain knowledge should keep that state outside the conversation, in Notion or a `memory` scope, not in shared context [I]. Claude Code's own guidance says to use skills to inject knowledge inline and subagents only for verbose output, tool restriction, or self-contained parallel work ([Subagents docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)) [P, vendor]. Her plan to spawn specialists as subagents "for cross-area work" is backwards on this reading: cross-area work is the dependent, sequential case that belongs in one context, and the independent per-area scans are the parallel case [I].

| Task type in her Hub | Pattern the evidence supports | Who writes |
|---|---|---|
| Each area scans its own inputs (inbox, portal PDFs, feeds) | Parallel, isolated, cheap model, 1-2k token summary back | Specialist writes only to its own Notion area page and the Idea Bench |
| Planning the week across areas | Single context, house manager | House manager |
| Priority changes, calendar, shared family plans, money | Single writer with her confirmation on high stakes | House manager, after her tap |
| Judging pitches against her goals | Separate clean-context arbiter with rubric | Arbiter proposes, she decides |

## Learning lives in notes, and the Idea Bench is already the proven shape

Because every routine run starts fresh, "continuous learning" cannot live in a specialist's personality; it lives in what the run reads first and writes last ([Routines](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)) [P]. Anthropic calls this structured note-taking and pairs it with just-in-time retrieval and compaction ([Anthropic context engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)) [P, vendor]. The native subagent `memory` loads only the first 200 lines or 25KB of MEMORY.md and tells the agent to curate it ([Subagents docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)) [P], so unbounded growth, stale facts and no invalidation are the predictable failure modes [I]. Notion already gives her a dated, human-visible record; the change is to make each specialist's notes page carry last-verified dates and get pruned, and to have the OpenClaw lesson in mind: a 50-day user reported silent context compaction losing details as a main breakage ([VelvetShark](https://velvetshark.com/50-days-with-openclaw)) [S].

The Idea Bench matches the only working discovery pattern we found. Operator's daily digest agent keeps a ledger, dedups by URL and near-duplicate headline, and holds a per-topic cap: "ask for three and you get the three best" ([operator.io](https://operator.io/blog/daily-news-digest-with-your-agent)) [S, vendor]. feed-digest writes her chat feedback to a file the next run reads so "tomorrow's digest adapts" ([feed-digest](https://github.com/TheExGenesis/feed-digest)) [P, README]. Her three-open-pitches rule is the same mechanism; what no one has added is tracking accept versus dismiss, which is the only way to learn whether a specialist is opening her eyes or wasting her attention [I]. For the "things she doesn't know" scans, the cheapest reliable targets are machine-readable: the MCP Registry API returns JSON with `updatedAt` timestamps and cursor pagination ([registry](https://registry.modelcontextprotocol.io/v0/servers?limit=1)) [P], and skills.sh publishes trending installs ([skills.sh](https://skills.sh)) [P]. Open web scanning inherits the failures Anthropic documented in its own research agents: preference for low-quality sources and inability to self-regulate effort ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)) [P, vendor].

## Pushback has no prior art; build it as a rubric critic and test for sycophancy

Nothing in the search found a chief-of-staff or goal-tree agent in production that tells its owner to change priorities [gap]. What exists is adjacent: evaluator-optimizer loops work when criteria are explicit ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)) [P]; reviewers given clean context catch more than those anchored to the author's reasoning ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working)) [S, vendor]; and in controlled multi-agent discussions, agents deferred to sycophantic peers until given sycophancy rankings, which raised accuracy 10.5 points ([arXiv 2604.02668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02668)) [P, preprint, six small open models]. Cognition also reports that small-to-large model asymmetry fails when the smaller model is not calibrated to escalate ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working)) [S, vendor], which argues for running the arbiter step on a stronger model than the scans [I].

Her Idea Bench's "honest case against" rule is the right instinct. The missing structure is that a specialist is structurally an advocate for its area and cannot also be the judge of whether Parenting should outrank Professional this month [I, untested by any source]. The fix is one arbiter step that reads only her top-level goals plus the open pitches, in a clean context, with a rubric that forces each pitch and each proposed priority shift to name the goal it serves, the goal it costs, and the evidence. Then test it: Anthropic's guidance is to start evals with about 20 cases using an LLM judge plus human review ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)) [P], and the specific thing to test is whether the arbiter agrees with her framing when it should not.

Her instinct to minimize scheduled runs is aimed at the wrong number. One daily routine is already far inside every stated cap, and the model-tier gap is only 2x to 4x on input price [I from the price table]. The expensive pattern is a daily run that spawns eight subagents, each loading a full skill, each returning a long report. The cheap version is a single routine on Haiku or Sonnet that reads a Notion "last scanned" date per area, fans out only to specialists whose inputs changed, caps each summary at 1-2k tokens and each specialist with `maxTurns`, and reserves a stronger model for the arbiter step [I; supported directionally by the sources above, unmeasured for her workload]. Routines include all connected connectors with write access by default, so she should remove the ones each routine does not need ([Routines](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)) [P]. Household-scale all-in costs are unmeasured anywhere; OpenClaw users report $6-13/month for personal use with outliers to $3,600/month driven by heartbeats and oversized personality files loaded every call ([Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/tutorials/openclaw-costs/), [BetterClaw](https://www.betterclaw.io/blog/openclaw-api-costs)) [S, vendor anecdotes].

## Conclusion

The question was whether the Life Hub is organized the right way, and the honest answer is that it is organized the way the best available evidence says a reading-heavy, single-decider system should be, while carrying one assumption that the evidence contradicts: that spawning more specialists more often produces more learning and more pushback. It produces more error amplification and more tokens. The three structural moves that follow are small: make the house manager the only writer, fan out conditionally on a cheap model, and insert a clean-context arbiter between the specialists' advocacy and her decisions. Notion and the Idea Bench need no redesign; they are the two parts of her plan that outside builders (Gainsbrugh, operator.io, OpenClaw users) arrived at independently after their own failures.

What changed in our understanding is the location of the frontier. The commercial market retreated to narrow, confirm-before-acting tools, the open-source movement reproduced her architecture with a worse security story, and the ad market sells the stack without the system of record. The one capability no one has shipped or measured is a specialist that tells its owner, with evidence, that her priorities are wrong. That is the part of the Life Hub that would be new, and the only way she will learn whether it works is to build the arbiter with a rubric, run twenty cases against it, and count how often it agrees with her when it should not.
